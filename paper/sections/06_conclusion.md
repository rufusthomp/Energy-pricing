# Conclusion

We set out to test whether wind and solar create storage value of different kinds: value
that any operator who knows the calendar can capture, and value that only an operator who
forecasts the day can reach. The data support a simpler and, for investors and
regulators, more useful conclusion. The renewable mix changes how much day-ahead storage
value there is. We find no evidence that it changes who can capture it.

The evidence has three layers, and they differ in strength.

- **Strongest: solar creates value, wind does not.** This was registered as an outcome
  without a predicted direction. It is large, precise, and survives every check in
  Section 6. The check that most weakens it compares zones only with their neighbours,
  and it still leaves about 60% of the effect. It fits what is known about solar's effect
  on the intraday profile [@bushnell2021] and extends it from prices to storage revenue.
- **Solid, with a qualification: solar makes the day more calendar-like.** It moves the
  daily peak and trough towards the hours the calendar predicts, by rank as well as by
  correlation. Wind's opposite effect on shape is registered and significant, but it is
  largely a multi-day weather effect, and its placebo test fails.
- **Negative: the value that requires information does not respond.** The calendar
  operator's euro shortfall is the same on sunny, windy and calm days. The registered
  capture-share test (H1) has the predicted sign but is inconclusive, and in euros it
  reduces to the scale result. The registered heterogeneity result (H4) is Denmark West.

This is not the paper we set out to write. The earlier single-market analysis that
motivated it found that decarbonisation shifts storage value towards sophisticated
operators, and that claim did not survive year fixed effects. The first draft of this
paper made a weaker version of the same claim, and it did not survive review. What
survives is less dramatic and better supported. For day-ahead arbitrage, the case for
storage in a solar-heavy system rests on value that simple schedules can capture. We
found no premium to forecasting sophistication that grows with wind.

**Implications, stated with their limits.** These results concern the day-ahead market
and a price-taking battery.

- An investor modelling day-ahead arbitrage revenue in a system adding solar can treat
  most of the increment as capturable by simple schedules. The share it will realise is
  better predicted by the level of value than by the generation mix.
- We find no evidence that wind-heavy systems favour operators with scale in forecasting
  at the day-ahead stage. That does not rule it out in the intraday and balancing markets
  where forecast errors are traded, which we do not model.

**Extensions.** Three follow from this design:

- **Intraday and balancing markets,** where wind's forecast errors are traded, and where
  any informational premium to wind is most likely to appear.
- **Weather-based treatments.** Numerical-weather-prediction or reanalysis irradiance and
  wind speed would remove the load denominator and the forecast-timing questions in
  Section 6, and turn "identification from weather" into a literal description.
- **An equilibrium treatment of storage entry.** A fleet large enough to flatten the solar
  trough would change the shape it trades on [@butters2025; @karaduman2023].
