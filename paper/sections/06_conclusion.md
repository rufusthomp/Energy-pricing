# Conclusion

This paper asked whether the two technologies of decarbonisation create storage value of
different kinds: value available to anyone who knows the calendar, or value available
only to those who forecast the day. Across 19 European bidding zones, with
identification from weather under zone-by-year, zone-by-month and date fixed effects, we
find three things.

1. **Solar creates arbitrage value, and wind does not.** A day with 10 percentage points
   more forecast solar offers 28% more perfect-foresight value and a €22/MWh wider
   spread. A windier day offers no more value and a slightly narrower spread, because
   wind lowers the whole price curve more than it reshapes it.
2. **Solar makes the day's price shape more typical, and wind makes it less typical.**
   Per standard deviation of weather, the two effects are equal and opposite. This is
   the mechanism the hypothesis proposed, and it is the paper's most precisely estimated
   result.
3. **Calendar-based operators capture a larger share of solar-driven value.** The
   difference between wind and solar has the predicted sign and a stable magnitude of
   about four to five points of capture per 10 pp. At the pre-registered specification
   it is only marginally significant (p = 0.10). It is stronger for persistence
   operators and in wind-dominated systems, where wind's informational cost emerges most
   clearly.

The sun, in short, behaves like a clock. Whether the wind behaves like a lottery depends
on how much of it a system has.

**Implications.**

- **Solar-dominated systems.** The storage value that decarbonisation creates in systems
  like Iberia, Greece and northern Italy is largely *public* in our sense: it recurs at
  predictable hours, so simple operators can capture most of it, and forecasting
  capability confers little advantage. Entry into storage there should face low
  informational barriers.
- **Wind-dominated systems.** In systems like Denmark, windy days erode what calendar
  rules capture, and the returns to forecasting rise with wind's share. There, the
  market structure of flexibility is more likely to favour operators with scale in data
  and trading. TSO forecasts, already published as a public good, are then worth more to
  small operators.

**Extensions.** Three follow directly from this design:

- **Intraday and balancing markets,** where wind's forecast errors are traded, and where
  its informational content is likely to be larger.
- **An equilibrium treatment of storage entry,** since a fleet large enough to flatten
  the solar trough would change the shape it trades on.
- **The Iberian gas cap of 2022–23,** a genuine natural experiment in price formation
  within the same data.
